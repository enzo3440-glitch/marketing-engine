## Don't
- No ad-speak questions ("Tired of…?", "Looking for…?"), no hype words (ultimate, game-changer, revolutionary), no
  exclamation marks, no emoji in on-screen text.
- No invented features, numbers, reviews or prices. Only what's real (see brand-content.md).
- Don't explain the product's internals (settings, toggles, menus). Say what it does for you and show the result.
- [Your own don'ts: words you hate, competitors you never name, topics you avoid.]

## Length, the look, and time to read
- Ads run 12-20 s; trial reels 8-20 s. The first 2 seconds have to work with the sound off.
- **Give every line time to be read.** On-screen text stays up at least 1.5 s, plus about 0.3 s per word beyond four
  (a 7-word line: 2.4 s). Never flash a line or cross-fade one line straight into the next. A screen with something to
  read holds 2.5-3 s. If it won't fit, cut a line, don't shorten the hold. The end card holds 2.5 s or more.

## The order of an ad (12-20 s)
1. **The hook (0-3 s).** The most striking real shot you have and the promise in one short line.
2. **The release (2-4 s).** Two or three quick shots of the better way. Don't hold back the good footage.
3. **How (3-5 s).** The product doing one thing that matters, big enough to read in one glance.
4. **The payoff (2-4 s).** The result, in real life. One short line or none.
5. **End card.** [Brand name], a short tagline, and one call to action naming a real action.

## The cinematic look
- Videos only for reels and video ads. Photos belong in carousels.
- No panning or zooming on photos. The only motion is real camera motion in real video.
- Text sits lower: hook lines around the middle of the frame (45-55% down), later lines in the lower third, never below
  ~80% down (the Instagram UI covers it) and never pinned to the top edge.
- Never show the same clip twice in one video.
- Cinematic grade: natural, filmic colour, soft contrast, gentle highlight roll-off, slightly lifted blacks, a light grain.
  Never oversaturated, neon or "HDR".
- Every on-screen word must make sense to a viewer: no editing notes, timestamps, file names or labels.
- [Your own look rules.]

## Check before it ships
1. Would someone watch the first 2 seconds with the sound off and want more?
2. Does it feel like "there's a better way", not "you're doing it wrong"?
3. Is every line short and something you'd actually say?
4. Does any shot or line need the viewer to know a product term? Cut it.
5. When the product is on screen, is it big enough to read in one glance?
